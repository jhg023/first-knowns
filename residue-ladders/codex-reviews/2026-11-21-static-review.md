# Residue ladders — static mathematical review

**Codex (OpenAI), 2026-11-21.** First review of engine v1 for A002226, A002227, A002228, A060363 and A060364. The repository was on `master` at `064f0f6302eeb184b66988a957805f6e78cf7257` with a dirty worktree. The entire project was untracked. The source hashes below identify the implementation reviewed.

## Assessment

The first changes I would make are to the campaign’s coverage and evidence handling. A bounded sweep can classify a published term above the requested limit. An unrestricted sweep omits a short suffix on **every** line at the numerical ceiling. A discovery can also reach the evidence ledger before a parity alarm invalidates its segment. These are conclusions from control flow and integer arithmetic. The project records no campaign or discovery affected by them. [Launcher](../launch.py#L480) · [Results](../RESULTS.md)

The lowest-cost performance experiment applies to **all five families**. A different initial value for the base-2 power saves five Montgomery squarings per sieve survivor at each frozen day height and eliminates the initial remainder. The larger research opportunity is specific to **A002226**. A bounded four-dimensional enumeration gives a complete set of representatives for every eligible prime. Reciprocity supplies explicit residue tables for that enumeration. Its work unit, duplicate handling and cost differ substantially from the present integer sweep. Neither proposal has a measured runtime here.

## Findings

### RSL-001 Complete the exact requested interval

**High priority · Observed in source/data and proved by index arithmetic.** The public limit is described as inclusive. `_words` rounds the permitted count up to a 64-candidate word. `window` handles every output and advances by the padded width. On a fresh quintic run, `--to 12` consequently handles $j=1,\ldots,64$, including the published primes 151 and 431. Conversely `--to 11` exits before processing the candidate 11 because the outer test uses `pos() >= limit`. Parsing the limit as `float` introduces another endpoint error for integers above $2^{53}$. [Arguments](../launch.py#L918) · [Word selection, classification and stop](../launch.py#L480)

At the hard ceiling $C=2^{63}$, `raw` correctly refuses a word that crosses $C$. Every fresh campaign window advances $j$ by a multiple of 64. The last legal index is $J_d=\lfloor(C-2)/(2d)\rfloor$, so $J_d\bmod64$ valid candidates remain when `_words` first returns zero. The stop reason nevertheless says the ceiling was reached. A legal short `raw_window` at the top can also call `raw` with one oversized word. The P1 ceiling test starts exactly a whole number of words from the end, so it misses this case. [GPU bounds and window slicing](../residue_gpu.py#L326) · [P1 construction](../residue_gpu.py#L476)

| $d$ | 5 | 7 | 11 | 13 | 17 |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|

**Handoff.** Use the exclusive index $E=1+\lfloor(\min(T,C-1)-1)/(2d)\rfloor$ for an inclusive integer limit $T$. An empty interval ends immediately. Parse decimal/scientific notation exactly, with an explicit policy for fractional input. Classify precisely `[next_j,E)` and commit the actual count. Near $C$, use a masked device tail that never forms an out-of-domain modulus, or shift a final legal word backwards and discard the overlap before classification. The CPU can also verify a terminal tail of at most 63 candidates. Keep survivor, composite and census counts consistent with the chosen route. In `_record`, move both confirmation windows left or clip them to the same legal domain. The present forward allowances of 499 and up to 4,383 indices beyond the candidate can fail before confirmation. Record the actual lengths and independently include the witness. [Confirmation windows](../launch.py#L289)

**Acceptance.** Exercise every final length from 0 to 63 for every family, limits just before/on/after a canary, one large limit that binary64 rounds, and a discovery-shaped confirmation at the final legal index. Compare split/resumed and one-piece **ordered outputs, counters and first unprocessed index**. Reuse an old actual `next_j` as the contiguous floor, including genuinely processed overrun. A historical ceiling message alone cannot certify the tail.

### RSL-002 Commit discovery evidence after the segment passes its checks

**High priority · Observed in source/data.** `_record` calls `evidence.record` and announces a first occurrence while `window` is still classifying the launch. The canary and sampled parity checks run later. If a check alarms, `campaign` saves the previous good boundary, but the evidence file and ledger row already exist. The three confirmations establish local membership and a local ordering check. They cannot establish that a smaller prime was retained elsewhere in the rejected segment. A replay which finds a smaller prime uses a different ledger key, so upserting the smaller $p$ leaves the stale claim for the same index. [Evidence write](../launch.py#L342) · [Boundary checks](../launch.py#L549) · [Alarm rollback](../launch.py#L633) · [Ledger keying](../../huntlib/evidence.py#L29)

**Handoff.** Stage all verified discoveries privately and advance a working frontier in increasing $p$, so later classifications in the segment still see the correct census floor. After segment checks pass, persist enough accepted state to finish evidence publication idempotently. Drain pending records on restart and clear them only after file and ledger agree. Announce and honor a discovery stop after that commit. Guard uniqueness by sequence and settled index as well as by $p$. Preserve every index of a plateau. When recovering a damaged ledger, reconstruct it from committed records or refuse the recovery explicitly. The current ledger read fallback starts an empty list on a parse error. [Working frontier](../launch.py#L215) · [Evidence persistence](../../huntlib/evidence.py#L29)

**Acceptance.** In scratch state, drop an earlier candidate, allow a later record to pass local confirmation, then trigger the segment alarm. The prior checkpoint and public evidence must agree afterward. Inject interruptions between acceptance, individual evidence writes, ledger writes and final checkpoint cleanup. Replay two finds and a plateau in one segment. Require exactly one consistent claim per index.

### RSL-003 Keep the interrupt checkpoint callback registered through shutdown

**High priority · Observed in source/data.** `Campaign.run` registers `_on_interrupt`, then removes it in `finally` as the exception unwinds. The outer `shutdown.graceful` catches `KeyboardInterrupt` only after that removal. The campaign’s save callback is therefore absent when `shutdown` invokes the remaining callbacks. Normal exit’s forced save is also skipped. An interrupt before the first ordinary save can leave no checkpoint. Later interrupts can resume from the previous on-disk save instead of the most recent completed segment. At the default settings, ordinary writes are throttled to about 30 seconds while boundaries target about three seconds. [Campaign save and callback](../launch.py#L432) · [Registration and removal](../launch.py#L523) · [Outer handler](../../huntlib/shutdown.py#L114)

**Backup-only recovery is part of the same test.** The shared writer rotates the live checkpoint to a backup before replacing it with the new file. An interrupt between the two renames can leave only the backup. Both the refusal check and the loader currently return early when the live path is absent. Fresh initialization can then restart at 1 with a valid boundary still on disk. On startup, apply the existing key policy to a backup-only state. Inject a stop between the renames and check recovery of a matching backup and refusal of a foreign one. [Checkpoint rotation](../../huntlib/checkpoint.py#L107) · [Load](../../huntlib/checkpoint.py#L238) · [Startup refusal](../../huntlib/checkpoint.py#L389)

**Handoff and acceptance.** Keep the callback live through the outer handler and the final save. Unregister it after normal completion and clean up completed in-process campaigns so a later interrupt cannot save a stale campaign. Continue to save the immutable boundary. Drive the real registration/handler path in a scratch process, interrupt after a second completed boundary but before its periodic save, then compare every durable field and resumed result with an uninterrupted run. Exercise interruption during the first segment and the forced save as well. Exit 130 alone cannot validate this behavior. The primorial-AP launcher is a relevant donor for boundary callbacks. ### RSL-005 Shorten the base-2 power on all five lines

**First performance experiment · Proved arithmetic equivalence · Unmeasured runtime.** Round 2 interleaved two dependent squaring chains per thread. The optimization log separately left division-free initialization open. Two implementations can be evaluated independently using the existing raw pass predicate, including on odd composites. [Device `rl_montmul` and `rl_test2`](../residue_gpu.py#L76) · [Optimization history](../OPTIMIZATION_LOG.md)

**Shared quotient.** Let $R=2^{64}$. When $\lfloor R/N_{\min}\rfloor=\lfloor R/N_{\max}\rfloor=Q$, monotonicity proves $R\bmod N=R-QN$ for every candidate in the launch. Compute the endpoint quotients on the host in wide integers. One unsigned low-word product and negation gives the canonical remainder for every odd $N>1$ there. This replaces the per-modulus initial remainder in both `rl_test2` and `rl_run`. The first full launch of the five day shapes has constant $Q$ equal to **217, 297, 108, 115 and 42** respectively. The opening shapes cross many quotient boundaries. Retain the ordinary path whenever the endpoint check fails. [Kernel initializers](../residue_gpu.py#L175) · [Shape coordinates](../score.py#L62)

**Inverse power.** For $N>2^{32}$ let $t=j+64$, $k=\operatorname{bitlength}(t)-6$, $h=\lceil t/2^k\rceil$, and $b=h2^k-t$. On these lines $k\ge0,\ 32\le h\le64,\ 0\le b<2^k$. Initialize $x=2^{64-h}$, a canonical word less than $N$. It already represents $R\,2^{-h}\bmod N$. Read exactly the $k$ low bits of $b$, leading zeros included. For each bit, Montgomery-square $x$, then double it modulo $N$ when the bit is one. A represented exponent $u$ becomes $2u+\mathrm{bit}$. Consequently the final word equals

$$
R\,2^{-h2^k+b}=R\,2^{-t}=2^{-j}\pmod N.
$$

Compare that word directly with 1 and $N-1$. Inversion fixes the set $\{1,-1\}$ for every odd modulus, so this preserves the **raw** base-2 pass stream, including the composites that alarm and parity paths exercise. Use the existing route for smaller moduli. All modular doubles fit because $2N<2^{64}$. Form $b$ and its mask using unsigned arithmetic and handle the paired lanes independently if their $k$ values differ. Squaring the shorter lane’s seed as padding would change its value. [Current paired exponent loop](../residue_gpu.py#L178)

|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Current squarings per sieve survivor | 52 | 51 | 52 | 52 | 53 |

**Previously measured here.** Round 1.2 measured the first test at 4.72 ms of an 8.26 ms quintic launch, at $8.5\cdot10^{16}$, with $2^{22}$ words before round 2’s paired chains. The five saved squarings remove about 9.4–9.8% of the *squaring count*. Treating that percentage as a runtime improvement would ignore setup, bit density, the rest of the pipeline and the change of engine. The quotient and inverse variants both remove the first test’s remainder, so their savings overlap. [Phase split and shape comparisons](../OPTIMIZATION_LOG.md)

**Acceptance.** Compare every raw base-2 pass, the sorted raw output and the host prime stream on common windows. Include $N$ on both sides of $2^{32}$, quotient transitions, odd composites, an unpaired lane, a pair straddling a power of two in $t$, $h=64$, $b=0$ and the ceiling. Require identical canaries, census and frozen fingerprints. Time quotient-only, inverse-only, and inverse with the quotient in the run kernel against v1 across all five DAY and OPEN shapes. Prefer the simplest arm whose end-to-end advantage exceeds interleaved baseline variation. These transformations preserve the cursor key.

### RSL-006 Build a bounded quintic residue sieve in four coordinates

**Research priority · Proved completeness and residue counts · Unmeasured cost.** The project already identified Eisenstein reciprocity and the unit-domain problem. The following construction supplies a finite box, the normalization needed by the reciprocity law, and small residue tables. It works for A002226. [Previously deferred field sieve](../OPTIMIZATION_LOG.md#unsearched-with-prices) · [Grant, *Geometric proofs of reciprocity laws*, Theorem 1](https://euclid.colorado.edu/~grant/publications/kummer.pdf) · [LMFDB field 4.0.125.1](https://www.lmfdb.org/NumberField/4.0.125.1)

1. **Bound the generators.** In $K=\mathbb Q(\zeta_5)$, every $p\equiv1\bmod5$ splits into four prime ideals of norm $p$. The field discriminant is 125 and the Minkowski bound $(4! /4^4)(4/\pi)^2\sqrt{125}<2$ proves each ideal principal. For a generator $\alpha$, let $A,B$ be its absolute values in the two complex pairs, ordered by the embeddings in which the real unit $\phi=(1+\sqrt5)/2$ has absolute values $\phi,\phi^{-1}$. Multiplying by a power of $\phi$ puts $1\le A/B<\phi^2$. Then $AB=\sqrt p$ and $A+B\le(\phi+\phi^{-1})p^{1/4}=\sqrt5\,p^{1/4}$.
2. **Choose a semi-primary associate.** Put $\lambda=1-\zeta_5$. Modulo $\lambda^2$ the generator is $s+t\lambda$ with $s\ne0\bmod5$. Multiplying by $\zeta_5^u$ replaces $t$ with $t-us\bmod5$, so one choice of $u\bmod5$ makes the generator congruent to a rational integer. This preserves $A,B$. Writing $\alpha=\sum_{i=0}^3c_i\zeta_5^i$, the resulting coefficient conditions are $\sum c_i\ne0\bmod5$ and $\sum i c_i=0\bmod5$. Their joint fraction is $4/25$.
3. **Enumerate a finite box.** Give the missing fifth coefficient the value $c_4=0$. Subtract the length-five inverse Fourier formula for $c_4$ from the formula for $c_i$. The zero-frequency coefficient sum cancels, and each remaining difference of roots of unity has absolute value at most 2. Thus $|c_i|\le4(A+B)/5\le(4\sqrt5/5)X^{1/4}$ for $p\le X$. Integer outward bounds can use $25c_i^4\le256X$. Enumerating this box suffices even without numerically comparing embedding ratios. Duplicate generators and the four conjugate ideals are handled by de-duplicating **prime norms**.
4. **Generate admissible residues.** For $q=2,3,7,13,17$, $q$ has order four modulo 5. Hence $\mathbb Z[\zeta_5]/q=\mathbb F_{q^4}$. Eisenstein reciprocity for the semi-primary generator gives the exact predicate $\alpha^{(q^4-1)/5}=1\bmod q$ for $q$ to be a fifth power modulo the prime norm $p$. Precisely $(q^4-1)/5$ of the $q^4$ coefficient residues pass. For 2 these are the three nonzero elements of $\mathbb F_4$. CRT therefore gives **48 of $6^4=1,296$** classes for 2 and 3, and **23,040 of $42^4=3,111,696$** after adding 7. Prime 5 is ramified and must be tested directly. All five selected wheel primes differ from every eligible quintic prime, so their zero classes give no equality exception here.

**Cost.** A full box through $X$ has leading size $163.84X$ before residue filtering. Applying the semi-primary condition and the 2, 3, 7 tables leaves a leading $0.1941X$ coefficient vectors. Adding 13 gives $0.03882X$, and adding 17 gives $0.007764X$. These are leading counts for complete CRT periods in a four-dimensional box, with boundary terms, duplicate norms and composites. The old line has $0.1X+O(1)$ integer positions before sieving and the recorded quintic day shape had about 10.9 survivors per 64 of those. Counting vectors makes clear why two or three small tables alone do not settle feasibility. Generating admissible vectors, computing their norms and closing thin norm intervals may dominate. A full-box count cannot be charged once per narrow campaign segment. [Existing sieve costs](../OPTIMIZATION_LOG.md#unsearched-with-prices)

**Exact norm and cost of closing an interval.** In cyclic indices modulo five, set $S_m=\sum_i c_i c_{i+m}$, $U=S_0-S_2$ and $V=S_1-S_2$. Then $\alpha\bar\alpha=U+V(\zeta_5+\zeta_5^{-1})$. Since $\zeta_5+\zeta_5^{-1}$ has minimal polynomial $t^2+t-1$, the rational norm is $U^2-UV-V^2$. This gives a short integer norm calculation for each admitted vector. With $|c_i|\le B$ and $c_4=0$, $|U|\le7B^2$ and $|V|\le6B^2$. The absolute sum of the three final products is at most $127B^4$, which fits signed 128 bits throughout the stated box and ceiling. The reference calculation can use an independent resultant. For fixed $c_1,c_2,c_3$, the norm is a monic quartic in $c_0$. Exact root isolation or integer monotonicity intervals can restrict $c_0$ to the required norm shell before applying residue tables. Rebuilding all three-coordinate slices for every short shell still costs on the order of $X^{3/4}$ per shell. In the full-box scenario above with 17 included, matching the v1 quintic DAY rate requires processing about **2.8 billion admitted vectors/s** before allowing time for generating them, sorting norms and confirming the segment. This break-even calculation uses the leading box count and the existing $3.602\cdot10^{11}$ line rate. Tighter geometry or fewer duplicate representatives can change it.

**Handoff and acceptance.** First compare a norm-bounded CPU enumeration, with exact integer norm/resultant arithmetic, to the independent original-definition oracle. Generate small CRT levels directly and stage larger prime conditions. Price generation, norm calculation, multiplicities, sorting, memory and completion of each numerical interval. Use a wheel condition only when its prime lies among the first `floor` bases. Thus 7 needs floor ≥4, 13 needs ≥6 and 17 needs ≥7. Use the direct engine for the early canaries. For every candidate norm in a segment $[L,H)$, check the numerical bounds, primality, the omitted bases including 5, and the full run. Use wide intermediates even when the retained norm fits 63 bits. Sort distinct prime norms before classifying or increasing the frontier. Close **all** coefficient slices that could hit $[L,H)$ before claiming leastness there.

Compare all mathematically eligible primes and the production census on identical intervals. The old intermediate sieve and base-2 counts have a different meaning. Version those counters and scoring separately, inherit only an already completed numerical floor, and keep unclosed slices and pending records private across restarts. Test equality at the norm boundaries, conjugates and associates, a plateau, every canary and an interrupted partially enumerated shell. Reject adoption if completeness fails, or if full norm-interval throughput and confirmation time lose to the integer engine. The other fields have degrees 6, 10, 12 and 16 and different unit ranks. This four-coordinate proof supplies no implementation or speed estimate for them.

## Work estimates, alternatives and execution record

**Previously measured here.** The benchmark ledger reports 2026-11-21 RTX 4090 rates for two $2^{29}$-candidate launches per shape. The OPEN shapes start near $2^{42}$ with shorter exponents. The DAY shapes use floors 6, 5, 4, 4 and 4 to keep their returned streams populated. At the recorded DAY rates a full launch spans roughly 10–15 ms of device time. The launcher targets three-second segments and checks a six-second elapsed guard between windows. Host work, parity, evidence and saves can extend those times. The project timed independent confirmation at about 1 ms and local re-derivation at 25 ms on selected inputs. A confirmed-stop estimate must include closure and durable publication. The historic day-target clocks of approximately 66, 28, 45, 35 and 69 hours are fitted-model estimates from the opening. This review establishes no new end-to-end speed or campaign ETA. [Benchmarks](../BENCHMARKS.md) · [Loop and confirmations](../OPTIMIZATION_LOG.md#the-loop) · [Model snapshot](../model_results.json)

**Why a sibling integer wheel cannot sieve this residue property.** Fix $d$ and any ordinary rational congruence modulus $M$, and consider a reduced class compatible with $p\equiv1\bmod d$. For base 2 the splitting field $K=\mathbb Q(\zeta_d,2^{1/d})$ has Galois group $C_d\rtimes(\mathbb Z/d)^\times$. The degree $d$ radical extension follows, for example, from valuation 1 at a prime of $\mathbb Q(\zeta_d)$ over the unramified prime 2. The commutator is $C_d$, so its largest abelian subfield is $\mathbb Q(\zeta_d)$. Its intersection with $\mathbb Q(\zeta_{\mathrm{lcm}(d,M)})$ is exactly that subfield. Frobenius in the chosen congruence class is compatible with both trivial and nontrivial translations in $C_d$. Chebotarev gives primes of both types. Thus no such class decides even the first base by itself. More generally the first $n$ rational prime bases are independent modulo $d$-th powers over $\mathbb Q(\zeta_d)$. The valuation over each base is 1, or $d-1$ for the ramified base $d$, both invertible modulo $d$. The same commutator argument for $C_d^n$ allows a fully passing prime in every compatible reduced class. This is an asymptotic existence argument and supplies no finite-interval lower bound. The rank-coordinate method in doubling-ladders transfers for *periodic divisibility* or for the coefficient tables in RSL-006. It cannot impose a power-residue condition on an ordinary integer wheel.
